A Consensus Cross-Species Catabolic Signature of Spaceflight-Induced Muscle Atrophy

A harmonised cross-species meta-analysis of spaceflight skeletal muscle transcriptomics (NASA OSDR + LINCS L1000), deriving a 141-gene consensus catabolic signature and translating molecular drivers into evidence-based plant nutritional countermeasures.

Abstract

Spaceflight-induced muscle atrophy remains a principal barrier to long-duration space exploration, and its molecular drivers are incompletely translated into actionable countermeasures. We performed a cross-species random-effects meta-analysis of 10 transcriptomic datasets from the NASA Open Science Data Repository (OSDR), encompassing rodent spaceflight (OSD-21, OSD-111, OSD-135, OSD-401), human cell culture spaceflight (OSD-684), human astronaut cfRNA (OSD-530, GSE213808), Inspiration4 crew blood transcriptomics (OSD-569, OSD-571), and ground-based bed rest analogs (OSD-370).

After mapping all genes to human orthologs, we derived a consensus catabolic signature of 141 differentially expressed genes (77 up-regulated, 64 down-regulated; |log2FC| > 0.25, adjusted p < 0.05) from 32,770 tested genes. The signature was dominated by mitochondrial electron transport chain (ETC) genes (MT-ND1 through MT-ND6, MT-CO2, MT-CYB, MT-ATP8; log2FC +1.23 to +1.59) and included the master mitochondrial biogenesis regulator PPARGC1A/PGC-1α (log2FC = −0.51, p_adj = 0.092) among down-regulated genes. Gene set enrichment analysis across 6,877 pathways revealed nominal enrichment of mitochondrial NADH-to-ubiquinone electron transport (NES = +1.45, p = 0.002) and mitochondrial translation (NES = +1.34, p = 0.011).

Connectivity mapping against LINCS L1000 perturbation signatures (559 compounds ranked, 182 clinically annotated) and a curated database of 31 nutritional supplements identified HMB, CoQ10, creatine, vitamin D3, and resveratrol as top counter-catabolic candidates. Nutritional translation using published amino acid composition data for 41 vegan protein sources identified pea protein isolate (leucine 6.50 g/100 g, MPS score 2.25) and soy protein isolate (leucine 6.60 g/100 g, MPS score 2.11) as optimal plant-based protein sources for meeting the 2.5 g leucine per meal threshold for muscle protein synthesis activation. Optimized meal combinations achieved >3.2 g leucine and >39 g protein per serving. These results provide a reproducible, data-driven framework linking spaceflight transcriptomics to evidence-based nutritional countermeasures applicable to both astronaut health and terrestrial disuse atrophy.

Actionable Dietary Countermeasures

Spaceflight induces severe catabolic signaling through UPS activation, mitochondrial decoupling, and suppressed mTOR phosphorylation. Based on LINCS L1000 connectivity scores, target coverage, and published clinical evidence, five primary dietary supplements were prioritized alongside plant-based protein optimization:

Top Candidate Supplements

Supplement Biological Role Molecular Target & Mechanism Daily Target Dosage
HMB (β-hydroxy β-methylbutyrate) Anti-catabolic leucine metabolite Suppresses ubiquitin-proteasome system (MuRF1/Atrogin-1) and stimulates mTORC1 protein synthesis. 3.0 g / day
Coenzyme Q10 (Ubiquinone) Mitochondrial electron carrier Restores electron transfer through ETC complex I/III, mitigating microgravity-induced ROS leakage. 100 – 200 mg / day
Creatine Monohydrate Phosphagen energy buffer Restores cellular phosphocreatine/ATP reserves, preserves muscle cell hydration and cross-sectional area. 5.0 g / day
Vitamin D3 (Cholecalciferol) Nuclear steroid hormone Binds VDR to regulate skeletal muscle contractility and sarcoplasmic reticulum Ca²⁺ homeostasis. 2,000 – 4,000 IU / day
Resveratrol (Polyphenol) Sirtuin-1 / AMPK activator Allosteric SIRT1 activation driving PGC-1α deacetylation and mitochondrial biogenesis restoration. 250 – 500 mg / day

Plant-Based Protein Sources & Leucine Threshold

To trigger Muscle Protein Synthesis (MPS), a per-meal threshold of 2.5 g leucine is required. Among 41 evaluated plant sources, isolated protein powders provide the highest leucine density and caloric efficiency:

Protein Source Leucine (g / 100g) Total Protein (g / 100g) MPS Score Portion for 2.5g Leucine
Pea Protein Isolate 6.50 g 80.0 g 2.25 38.5 g (154 kcal)
Soy Protein Isolate 6.60 g 88.0 g 2.11 37.9 g (144 kcal)
Spirulina (Dried) 4.94 g 57.5 g 1.84 50.6 g (147 kcal)
Hemp Protein Powder 3.30 g 50.0 g 1.45 75.8 g (303 kcal)
Nutritional Yeast 3.40 g 50.0 g 1.42 73.5 g (257 kcal)

Dietary Takeaway: Whole plant foods (lentils, beans, tofu) provide crucial micronutrients and dietary fiber, but require high caloric intake to reach 2.5g leucine. In spaceflight or clinical disuse, combining whole foods with 25–30g of pea or soy protein isolate easily delivers >3.2g leucine and >39g protein per meal within strict caloric limits.
